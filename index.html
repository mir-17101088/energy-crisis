The Grid That Ran Out of Everything at Once

A war closed a strait. Then a fire on a moored ship took the lights out. How it connected.

A war closed a strait. The strait emptied a pipeline. The pipeline failed on a ship moored off Moheshkhali. By August load shedding was running past 3,000 megawatts and the country was meeting 57 percent of its gas demand. This is how it connected.

The Global Shockwave and the Domestic Trap

Two things happened to Bangladesh in 2026. One was sudden and foreign. The other had been arriving slowly for seven years.

The slow half of the story

Imported LNG did not supplement domestic gas. It replaced it.

While Qatar was cutting, Bangladesh's own gas fields were already emptying. Domestic production has fallen for seven straight years.

The valve closes

The terminal shut down. Regasification stopped. Four hundred and fifty million cubic feet a day left the system.

The number

That was 17 percent of all the gas in the country. Half the LNG.

National supply fell from 2,620 to 2,170 million cubic feet a day.

Fifty seven percent

The country met 57 percent of demand.

The gap is 1,630 MMcfd, derived by subtracting supply from the 3,800 MMcfd demand figure.

The grid takes the hit

Power plants had been getting about 900 million cubic feet a day. They dropped to 650. Gas generation fell from 5,200 megawatts to 3,500. The board started burning oil instead.

What it cost

Gas power cost about Tk 31 crore a day. Oil power cost Tk 90 crore and produced less than half as much.

Per megawatt generated, furnace oil ran roughly six times the cost of gas. Every hour the terminal stayed down, the state bought the same electricity six times over.

This keeps happening

The July failure was not an accident of bad luck. Bangladesh has two floating terminals. In three years, four separate events have knocked out one or both.

A two-node import system has no redundancy by construction. Losing one node removes about half the imported supply, every time, whatever the cause. Cyclone, maintenance, or fire, the failure mode is identical. This is a design property, not a run of bad luck.

The Economic Pressure Cooker

Gas does not arrive as a quantity. It arrives as a pressure. Below a certain pressure, a factory is not slow. It is stopped.

Below the pressure a process needs, output does not fall. It stops.

A ceramic kiln needs 30 to 35 pounds per square inch. A knitwear dyeing line needs at least 10. A CNG pump needs 7 or 8. In late July almost nobody got what they needed. Every row is on the same scale: the bar is what arrived, the red mark is what the process requires.

Where a figure was reported as a range, the bar runs to the top of the range. Industry associations and company officials

Pressure failures are not proportional. A dyeing line running at a fifth of the pressure it needs does not produce a fifth of its normal output. It produces ruined fabric. Ceramics puts it plainly: yield falls from 90 percent to 10.

The diesel arithmetic

Gas electricity cost Tk 14 to 15 a unit. Diesel cost Tk 34. Switching raised running costs about 20 percent.

The exporters' stated fear was not the cost. It was delivery dates and buyer confidence. Cost is recoverable next quarter. A buyer who reroutes an order to Vietnam may not come back.

Transport and the queue

CNG stations run on pipeline pressure. When pressure fell to nearly zero, the queues stopped moving. One driver in Tejgaon waited sixteen hours from four in the morning.

Nine price changes in four months, and cooking gas moved both ways

Between April and August the state changed the price of energy nine times. Fuel went up twice. Electricity went up once and was partly walked back the next day. Cooking gas went up, up, down, down, then up.

Energy and Mineral Resources Division and BERC orders

Fuel prices rose on 18 April even as Brent fell 9 percent to $90.

The 18 April hike is the tell. Crude was falling. The government raised prices anyway, mid month, breaking its own monthly adjustment rule. It was the first mid month change since the automatic mechanism began in 2024. The trigger was not the world price. It was the accumulated loss on the state's own books, and the point at which it could no longer be carried.

Chattogram generated nearly 3,000 megawatts at peak and could not keep its own lights on. Local plants feed the national grid. The region then waits to be allocated power back from it.

The Human Toll

Load shedding is a scheduling word. What it means is a five year old who cannot sleep.

My little son cries through the night. I have no choice but to sit beside him and fan him manually for hours.
Shuvechchha GhoshResident, Askar Dighir Par, Chattogram

Seven or eight outages a day, dawn to midnight. 33 degrees, 69 percent humidity. The forecast officer noted people only feel comfortable below 50 percent.

Dhaka got the largest total shortfall. Rural districts got the longest hours. In parts of Bagmara, electricity was gone for about sixteen hours a day.

The stated rationale is that private imports reduce the subsidy burden. That does not follow. The subsidy exists because fuel sells below import cost. A private importer held to the same regulated price needs the same support. One free to set its own price shifts the burden to consumers. Either way the cost does not vanish. It moves.

The counter-argument

A single state buyer is a single point of failure in procurement, exactly as a single terminal is in supply. Competition could improve resilience even where it does not cut cost.

By 10 August one boiler was back. Supply had climbed to about 2,350 million cubic feet. Demand was still 3,800. The shortfall had not closed. It had narrowed.
